01 / The Problem

Threats don't announce themselves.

VOLUME

Constant flow

Networks carry continuous traffic; reviewing it by hand does not scale.

VARIETY

Evolving attacks

Attack behaviour changes, so fixed patterns can fall out of date.

SPEED

Short response window

The longer a threat goes unnoticed, the more room it has to cause harm.

02 / Motivation & Problem Statement

Detection needs to learn, not just match.

PROBLEM STATEMENT

How can network traffic be analysed automatically so that suspicious behaviour is identified and surfaced to a user quickly, with a pathway toward automated response?

Manual monitoring

Depends on analyst attention and is difficult to sustain across large volumes of traffic.

Fixed rules & signatures

Effective against known patterns, but need ongoing updates for new or modified attacks.

Alerts without context

Raw alerts alone do not tell a user what to look at first.

04 / Existing Approaches

Each approach has a role — and a trade-off.

Signature-based

Match known patterns

Strength

Reliable and well understood for known attacks.

Limitation

Needs updated signatures; may miss novel variants.

Anomaly-based

Flag deviations

Strength

Can surface behaviour not seen before.

Limitation

Unusual-but-legitimate traffic can raise false alarms.

ML-based · SENTINEL direction

Learn from labelled data

Strength

Can learn patterns that are hard to write as explicit rules.

Limitation

Depends on data quality and may generalise poorly to unseen environments.

General characterisation of approaches; real deployments often combine several.

12 / Security, Limitations & Challenges

Honest about the hard parts.

01

False positives

Legitimate traffic may be flagged, creating alert fatigue.

02

Dataset bias

A model reflects the data it learned from, including its gaps.

03

Generalisation

Performance on a dataset may not carry over to a different network.

04

Performance

Analysis must keep pace with traffic to be useful in practice.

05

Evolving attacks

New behaviours may require retraining and monitoring.

06

Detection ≠ prevention

Flagging a threat is separate from reliably blocking it; prevention is still planned work.
